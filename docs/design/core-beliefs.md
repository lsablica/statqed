# Core Beliefs

Status: **Draft**.

1. A statistical output is not a statistical claim until its target, design/model, procedure, assumptions, randomness, and interpretation are explicit.
2. Identification, inference, numerical correctness, data binding, and interpretation are separate arguments.
3. Formal proof establishes conditional implications; it does not make empirical premises true.
4. The best trusted computing base is smaller than the analysis stack. Fast external systems should produce checkable witnesses.
5. A language frontend is useful only when it preserves semantics and exposes unsupported behavior.
6. Exact finite-sample results are especially valuable early because their proof and certificate boundaries are clean.
7. Asymptotic results remain important, but the artifact must preserve convergence mode, quantifiers, and the absence of a finite-n error guarantee.
8. Diagnostics remain diagnostics unless a theorem licenses a stronger inference.
9. Public theorem statements are governed interfaces. Proof automation is abundant; semantic review is scarce.
10. Source fidelity, non-vacuity, counterexamples, and interpretation review are part of correctness.
11. Cross-language agreement must be tested at the canonical IR/byte level, not assumed from similar syntax.
12. Stable identifiers, open formats, archival verification, and contributor credit are academic infrastructure.
13. Negative results and discovered errors are valuable outputs.
14. General mathematics belongs upstream when feasible; StatQED should specialize in statistical semantics and composition.
15. Agents may accelerate production but remain untrusted and must operate through contracts, reviews, and reproducible evidence.
